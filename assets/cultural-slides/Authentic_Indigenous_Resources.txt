Slide 3: Defining Authentic Indigenous Resources (FNESC)
What Are Authentic First Peoples Texts?
Authentic First Peoples texts are historical or contemporary texts that
are created by First Peoples or through the substantial contributions of First Peoples
depict themes and issues that are important within First Peoples cultures (e.g., identity, tradition, role of family, importance of Elders, connection to the land, the nature and place of spirituality as an aspect of wisdom, the relationships between individual and community, and the importance of oral tradition — see Common Themes in First Peoples Texts later in this Introduction)
incorporate First Peoples story-telling techniques and features as applicable (e.g., circular structure, repetition, weaving in of spirituality, humour).

Slide 6: Avoiding Cultural Appropriation
Chrona defines: Appreciation vs Appropriation
Cultural Appreciation: Involves learning about and understanding other cultures through intentional sharing and respectful engagement.
Cultural Appropriation: Occurs when non-Indigenous individuals take elements of Indigenous knowledge without permission, often without recognizing its origins or context. This is particularly problematic when there's a power imbalance between cultures or a history of cultural suppression.

Slide 8: Professional Standards for BC Educators
Standard 9
Educators respect and value the history of First Nations, Inuit and Métis in Canada and the impact of the past on the present and the future. Educators contribute towards truth, reconciliation and healing. Educators foster a deeper understanding of ways of knowing and being, histories, and cultures of First Nations, Inuit, and Métis.
Educators critically examine their own biases, attitudes, beliefs, values and practices to facilitate change. Educators value and respect the languages, heritages, cultures, and ways of knowing and being of First Nations, Inuit and Métis. Educators understand the power of focusing on connectedness and relationships to oneself, family, community and the natural world. Educators integrate First Nations, Inuit and Métis worldviews and perspectives into learning environments.

Slide 9: Authentic Indigenous Resources & BC Standard 9
Choosing authentic Indigenous resources directly supports BC’s 9th Professional Standard for educators.
Standard 9 calls on teachers to respect and value Indigenous histories and the impact of colonization.
It also requires the integration of Indigenous knowledge and perspectives in all aspects of learning.
Using authentic resources ensures Indigenous voices are accurately and respectfully represented.
It helps move practice beyond tokenism and toward meaningful inclusion and reconciliation.
Teachers have a professional responsibility to critically select materials that align with these values.

Slide 10: Evaluate Your Resources with Jo Chrona’s Criteria
Take a few minutes to review your materials. Pair up and discuss what you notice. Where might changes or additions be needed?
Use Jo Chrona’s guiding questions (p.179) to evaluate their authenticity and cultural integrity:
1. Who created this resource?
Is it by an Indigenous author or in collaboration with Indigenous communities?
2. Whose voice is centered?
Does it reflect the lived experiences and perspectives of Indigenous Peoples?
3. What permissions were given?
Has the cultural knowledge been shared with consent and respect?
4. Is it community-specific?
Does it avoid pan-Indigenous generalizations and represent specific Nations or groups?
5. What messages does it convey?
Does it reinforce stereotypes or showcase the contemporary realities and strengths of Indigenous Peoples?
